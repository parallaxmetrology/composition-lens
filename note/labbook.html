1. Summary and claims ledger

The claim, in short. The height of the strongest horizontal line and the position of the strongest vertical line in a photograph can be read by a plain, named measurement (no model trained on photographs), and that measurement agrees with people. On CADB (9,497 photographs, read once) the horizon reading lies within 0.05 of the image height of the line the annotators drew for 53% of images (median error 0.040, against 0.226 when the marks are shuffled), and the vertical reading agrees similarly. The same readings separate PICD's horizon-in-the-middle from horizon-on-a-third categories (AUC 0.78 on fresh images) and the vertical-line pair (0.79), where whole-image methods stayed near chance. Not established: subject placement. The compact-object reading did not follow the annotators' subject boxes on CADB, the thirds-point and centre-point scores are barely above chance there, and the diagonal, golden, spiral and perspective scores are not supported on real photographs.

Background for a new reader. Photographers describe composition with named patterns: a horizon in the middle or on a third of the frame, a subject at the centre or at a rule-of-thirds point, a diagonal. Two public datasets label photographs this way. PICD (Zhao et al., CVPR 2025) gives each photograph one of 24 expert-assigned categories. CADB (Zhang, Niu and Zhang, BMVC 2021) gives each photograph one or more of 13 composition classes and the annotators' own drawn marks (lines, boxes). A reading here is a named number computed from an image with no training, such as "the strongest horizontal line sits at 0.37 of the height". The work asks whether such readings track what people mark and label, where they do, and where they don't. Every stage was fixed in a written plan before it ran, and the real-photograph tests were read once under a hashed specification.

Question. Photographers and artists use a small set of named compositions (thirds, phi, diagonals, golden triangle, rabatment, spiral, one- to three-point perspective). As far as a limited GitHub search found (it was rate-limited), public tools score one or two of them with a heuristic and none reports, per template, how far the measurement can be trusted. Can a named, inspectable measurement of each template be built, and where does it work?

Where it stands. Established (two datasets). The dominant horizontal and vertical line readings agree with the annotators' marked lines on CADB, rank the CADB horizontal and vertical classes above the rest (AUC 0.77 and 0.63), and separate the PICD horizon and vertical-line pairs on three sets. A trained probe with position-aware features matches or passes them given labelled images, so their advantage is that they need none. Held back. The compact-object reading separates P-RoT from P-Cent on PICD (AUC 0.83 on fresh images) but did not follow CADB's annotated subject boxes. Modest. 8 of 11 crosswalk predictions are met on each PICD set, with AUCs of 0.63 to 0.82; on CADB thirds and centre are 0.57 and 0.55. Not supported on real photographs. Diagonal, golden ratio, golden triangle, spiral and perspective scores. Synthetic. On exact layouts the catalogue of 23 templates (13 position templates implemented, the rest built or planned) is located to about 0.2% of the frame, and the engine reports which templates it cannot separate. As a classifier of dataset categories the lens is weaker than a fitted DINOv3 probe, which leads on 24 of 24 PICD categories.

How to read this book. PICD is a public benchmark of 24 photographic composition categories (Zhao et al., CVPR 2025); each image carries one expert label. A template is a named geometry (for example, lines at 1/3 and 2/3 of the frame). The lens measures how well an image's lines or objects sit on each template, in numbers that are named and can be inspected. AUC (one-vs-rest) is the chance that a random image of the target category scores higher than a random image of any other category: 0.5 is no separation, 1 is perfect. Pair AUCs compare two categories only; the pair accuracy reported in an earlier benchmark study (unpublished) is a different statistic, so they are not compared directly. Training-free means no model is fitted to labelled images (two detection thresholds were calibrated on synthetic images); a fitted layer is a logistic regression trained on labelled images, and is named as such wherever it appears. Design / confirmation / fresh: DevA (1,656 images) was used to design, DevB (1,656) was read once after the spec was frozen, LockboxB (960) was read once under a spec hashed beforehand. z and rank scores: how far a template's position stands above the rest of the row profile, by magnitude and by rank.

2. Aims, and what changed

The work started from an earlier, unpublished benchmark study of PICD, where five category pairs resisted every method and the data could not say why. The idea: build detectors for the named templates themselves, give each a null and a recovery test, and validate against labelled data. The plan (PLAN.md) fixes definitions and tests before each stage runs; changes after a result are dated amendments.

Plan changes after seeing results

A1 (20 Nov 2026), point evidenceAfter the first run, line images were being read as point structures (a line passes through the power points). Point evidence changed from the raw disc share to a blob response: the disc share minus the ring mean (first try, little effect), then minus the ring maximum (kept). Reason: a line has equally high share on both sides along it; a compact structure exceeds all its neighbours.

A4 (20 Nov 2026), within-kind accuracy (exploratory)Top-1 over all templates is confounded across kinds: a compact object on a power point also makes the line profiles peak. Top-1 within a kind (horizontal, vertical, point, diagonal) is reported next to it. Added after seeing R2, so labelled exploratory.

Evidence. At a long side of 384 px: a position profile of line strength against position for horizontal, vertical and the two diagonal orientations; a mass map and a blob response for compact objects; coverage of the dominant line (how much of its length has an edge). Fit. A template's fit is the profile value within a tolerance (1.5% of the frame) of its positions, ranked against the rest of the profile (rank score) and scored by magnitude against the profile's own spread (robust z, corrected for the number of primitives). No trained model is used anywhere in the lens. Full definitions: PLAN.md.

4. Synthetic tests

All stages were tested first on images whose layout is exact by construction. Sanity gates were fixed beforehand and all passed (position error under 1% of the frame, AUC at least 0.9 against nulls, kind accuracy at least 0.9, spiral AUC at least 0.9, perspective count accuracy at least 0.8).

Triangle, rabatment, perspective. The golden triangle separates from a bare diagonal at AUC 1.00; rabatment is the top template on 4:3 ({'rabatment': 40}) and ties with thirds on 3:2 and with the fifths end lines on 4:5; the exact vanishing-point count is 0.95 on clean line drawings (one-point scenes 0.90, median vanishing-point error 0.005 of the frame for one-point scenes).

5. Real photographs (PICD)

Design on DevA; spec frozen and hashed; one confirmation run on DevB; a last fresh test on LockboxB with the plan and predictions fixed before it was read. The comparators are a reimplementation of Facet's thirds and centre heuristic, a fitted logistic probe on the lens features (a fitted layer) and a fitted logistic probe on DINOv3 embeddings (a fitted layer over a trained model).

What the lens reports

6. The five pairs that no whole-image method separated

In an earlier benchmark study (unpublished) five category pairs scored 0.49 to 0.57 pair accuracy for every method. Three of them have a position reading. Two use the line readings (LS-Hori2 vs LS-Hori3, L-Ver2 vs L-Ver3), which section 7 validates against human-marked lines on a second dataset. The third (P-RoT vs P-Cent) uses the compact-object reading, which that check did not support, so it is shown here but held back. The two P-Dia pairs need an arrangement reading the lens does not have. The reading is the distance of the dominant horizontal line, vertical line or compact blob from the centre; the interpretation rule was fixed before any real image was read.

Reading the three figures together. The labelled categories do differ in position as defined, and a named reading shows it with no labelled images. Position-aware probes show the information is also easy to learn, so the separation is cleaner than the reading alone suggested (AUC up to 0.93 on LockboxB for the compact pair, where a patch-norm probe does much better than the blob reading). The pooled embedding discards much of it, though less on horizons (0.74) than on the other two pairs (0.64, 0.57).

7. A second dataset: CADB

CADB (Zhang, Niu and Zhang, BMVC 2021) has 9,497 photographs, 13 composition classes per image (multi-label) and the annotators' own marks for each class. It was downloaded after the plan (PLAN.md, section M4) was written and the spec hashed, and read once, with no design step: the lens is the same frozen lens. Two questions: do the template scores rank the CADB classes they map to, and do the lens's line positions agree with where the annotators drew the lines?

Reading. The pre-stated rule (at least four of five predicted classes above chance) reads "supports generalisation", but the result is lopsided. The line-coverage readings are strong (horizontal 0.77, vertical 0.63) and agree with the annotators' marks; the thirds and centre scores are barely above chance (0.57, 0.55); the diagonal, golden, spiral and perspective scores are not supported. The Facet-style thirds heuristic (0.59) is above the lens on rule_of_thirds. A fitted DINOv3 probe leads on every class.

9. Limits and threats to validity

  • CADB classes are multi-label and imbalanced (centre 4,804 images, radial 42); several classes have few test positives, so their intervals are wide. CADB images come from AADB (Flickr, research use only) and are not shown in this book.
  • The first M4 extraction hung and produced nothing; the extraction script was made crash-safe and the spec re-frozen before the rerun (amendment A6). The measurements are unchanged.
  • Eleven one-sided predictions were tested without multiplicity correction; the intervals are descriptive. DevA, DevB and LockboxB are all PICD images from the same sources and labelling scheme, so agreement across them is replication within one dataset, not independence of provenance.
  • Position tolerance (1.5% of the frame) and the 384-pixel working size are design choices fixed before the tests, not tuned.
  • Everything real was measured on one dataset (PICD) whose labels are the dataset's own; the crosswalk from templates to categories was made by name, not validated independently.
  • Synthetic tests use drawn strokes and tone splits on smooth backgrounds; they show the engine does what it is defined to do, not that photographs behave that way.
  • With 40 images per category on LockboxB, one AUC carries an interval of about +/- 0.1, so claims are about direction and ordering.
  • The three position pairs were chosen because they were the pairs that earlier instruments failed on; the pre-stated rules and the confirmation and fresh sets limit tuning, not that selection.
  • The DINOv3 probes use one model, whole-frame squashing to the model's input size, and logistic regression with cross-validated strength; other pooling or models could do better.
  • The Facet-style comparator is a reimplementation from the public source, not Facet's code, and runs at 384 pixels.
  • Thresholds tau_c and tau_b were calibrated on synthetic images; their transfer to photographs is untested except through the pair results, which do not depend on tau_b.
  • Fitted comparators (the logistic probes) are fitted layers and are named so; the lens readings and templates are training-free.
